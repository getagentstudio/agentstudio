What actually ran

One real native Codex conversation was exercised on the existing configured macOS runtime. No AgentStudio Rust Host was built or replaced. The question was whether an active native command and turn could finish after losing their requesting CLI, while another connection retained custody.

The successful causal sequence

Arm the observer before dispatch. Capture the new native turn and its exact pipe-read command.

Kill only the requesting child CLI. Wait for its actual exit with signal SIGKILL before releasing the private value.

The same command returns output matching the marker after trimming, with exit 0. The same native turn then completes, without another prompt.

The corrected driver returned supported-primitive with exit 0. The observer stayed connected throughout and exited after settlement. It was part of the prototype execution custody; its presence could keep the native thread loaded.

What this changes in the design

The result supports separating a requesting frontend's lifetime from continuing native execution custody. It gives the design a real local primitive to build on. The selected product obligation still requires an actual Host upgrade to preserve the active turn through the complete installed lifecycle.

Replacement-adapter reattachment, last-subscriber loss, independently attested provider-process incarnation or an already-blocked syscall, pending approval handover, durable standalone-task recovery, Linux/iOS, remote transport and other providers were not established. The source-level stale-turn cancellation gap was not exercised or repaired.
