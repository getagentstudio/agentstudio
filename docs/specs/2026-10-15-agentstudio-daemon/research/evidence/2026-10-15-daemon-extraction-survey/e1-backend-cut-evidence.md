## Legal orderings relevant to the cut

These are deductions from U1–U3/U8, not observed executions. A=accept, P=parse one PTY read, O=append its transformed Output, I=handle Init, C=handle Capture, F=handle Info; R=restore Output. Successful P→O is one synchronous section.

1. If accept and PTY readiness are present together: **A → P → O(new client)**; that client's I/C/F cannot dispatch until a later iteration. Further P→O pairs may precede its first request.
2. If PTY is not ready after A, I/C/F may be handled next iteration before the next P, provided no PTY readiness is then reported. Previously accumulated VT state can still precede A.
3. If PTY and that existing client's request are ready together: **P → O → request**. Thus pre-Init live bytes can also be represented in R; R is not a deduplicated suffix.
4. Complete same-connection **I → C → F** already buffered is handled contiguously. If election occurs, an empty Resize control is queued before optional R, then Capture, then Info; earlier queued Output remains ahead. No PTY read/client switch interleaves inside that batch.
5. If those requests span reads/iterations, P→O may occur between them. C can therefore describe state after shell redraw, whereas same-batch C describes state before that redraw is parsed. Response transmission can be partial/delayed without changing frame append order.
6. Different clients are dispatched in reverse current array order, not network arrival order. Another client's earlier-dispatched Resize, Init or injected Output can change VT before C; a later-dispatched one changes it after C. Injected Output parses/broadcasts synchronously in that dispatch and bypasses the PTY OSC133 rewrite. Input can change leadership immediately but its PTY effects need later write/read stages.
7. **C on connection X → attach/Init on Y** gives neither a common output queue nor a shared cut. Even on one connection, Info received after C is a request-order/capability observation, not a claim about another connection or downstream rendering.
8. Kill/Detach, client I/O error/EOF, PTY EOF and the U8 allocation/write failures can end or truncate these paths. PTY EOF ends the loop after the current iteration; a final flush is not guaranteed to drain all queued data. Their missing/partial outcomes cannot be scored as a successful checkpoint.

## Unresolved cut and observer conditions for Lead disposition

- The restoration profile must identify first attach versus reattach, whether restore actually occurred, active viewport/leader state, the backend mutation sources allowed during the cut, selected Capture rows/format, exact parsed chunk boundaries, and which pre-reply bytes are represented again. Source ordering narrows these conditions but supplies no executed closure.
- A Capture frame can delimit same-client queued bytes; it does not identify unfinished parser residue, prove ignored/failed writes absent, preserve every required screen/metadata element, or certify a fresh consumer's continued equivalence. The restore/live hypothesis remains **unresolved**, not refuted for every profile.
- OSC133 rewriting and automatic DA scanning operate on the supplied chunk without a cross-chunk tracker. Complete/split sequences can therefore have different transformed/reply behavior. The raw backend VT and transformed client stream are different observations; applying the same faulty transform to both references could conceal a defect.
- An Init leader, follower and silent no-Init observer have different resize/query-return behavior. No-Init observer must not be described as intrinsically read-only; no final product controller policy follows.
